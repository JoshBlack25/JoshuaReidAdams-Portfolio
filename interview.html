STAR reflections

SituationSI took part in a simulated ICT job interview as part of my preparation for Work Integrated Learning (WIL).
TaskTI had to present myself professionally, answer structured questions, and show confidence in both technical and behavioural answers.
ActionAI reviewed common ICT interview questions and structured my answers with the STAR method. I focused on clear communication, professional body language, staying calm, and linking answers to real examples from customer service and my development projects.
ResultRMy answers became more structured and confident. I improved at explaining technical concepts and behavioural situations clearly without rushing.
SituationSI had to build a digital portfolio with GitHub as part of my academic assessment.
TaskTCreate a structured, professional portfolio with my CV, projects and reflective learning evidence.
ActionAI started with Markdown pages and published them with GitHub Pages. For Project Presentation 3 I rebuilt the site as a custom Jekyll layout with reusable components, so it now holds my Agile eWIL evidence as well.
ResultRI understand how developers structure and publish an online portfolio, and I am more confident with version control, documentation and front-end design.
SituationSAcross several ICT projects I had to keep learning new tools, languages and problem-solving techniques.
TaskTImprove my technical ability while managing deadlines, debugging errors and meeting project requirements.
ActionAI worked iteratively: build small components, test them, find the errors, then fix and improve. I also drew on my academic and workplace experience to guide decisions.
ResultRI became more confident at problem-solving, more patient with debugging and more structured in how I approach development tasks.
Closing reflection

Skill development is not instant.

It is built through repetition, correction and persistence over time. Progress in development is rarely loud: it is steady, disciplined and continuous.
